PRD — PRAVAH Causal Risk Intelligence Engine

Version: 1.0 Prepared for: Smart India Hackathon 2026 Problem Statement
ID: SIH26165 Theme: Smart Automation PS Category: Software Team: Nexora

1.  PRODUCT OVERVIEW

PRAVAH is a Causal Risk Intelligence Engine designed to analyze OIL’s
Unsafe-Act/Unsafe-Condition (UA/UC), near-miss, and incident reports.
Its purpose is to move beyond simple text classification and reconstruct
the underlying risk represented in each report.

The core causal structure is:

Activity → Hazardous Energy → Barrier Failure → Potential Consequence

Using this structure, PRAVAH supports three critical safety decisions: •
SIF Potential • Life-Saving Rule (LSR) • Recurring Precursor Pattern

The product connects differently worded reports that describe the same
underlying failure mode and prioritizes where HSE action matters most
using exposure-weighted SIF density. It also incorporates
human-in-the-loop feedback so expert reviews can continuously calibrate
the system for real-world deployment.

2.  PROBLEM STATEMENT

Safety organizations receive large volumes of free-text UA/UC,
near-miss, and incident reports. Important risk signals may be hidden
within varied language, scattered observations, and reports that
describe similar failures using different words.

The key product problem is therefore not only understanding what a
report says, but identifying the risk structure behind it and surfacing
actionable patterns.

PRAVAH addresses: • Hidden high-consequence potential that may not be
reflected by reported severity. • Recurring barrier failures that are
difficult to see across differently worded reports. • Risk concentration
that may be obscured by high report volume alone. • Manual screening
effort required to identify the most important reports. • The need for
explainable AI outputs that HSE decision-makers can validate.

3.  PRODUCT VISION

Turn existing safety reports into structured, explainable causal risk
intelligence without disrupting the organization’s existing reporting
workflow.

4.  PRODUCT GOALS

Primary goals: 1. Reconstruct causal risk from free-text safety reports.
2. Identify potential Serious Injury & Fatality (SIF) exposure. 3. Link
reports to relevant Life-Saving Rules. 4. Detect recurring precursor and
barrier-failure patterns. 5. Prioritize HSE attention using risk-focused
signals rather than report volume alone. 6. Provide causal evidence
behind every important AI-generated flag. 7. Improve performance through
expert review and feedback. 8. Integrate with the existing UA/UC,
near-miss, and incident reporting workflow.

5.  NON-GOALS

The provided product concept does not specify: • Replacing OIL’s
existing reporting process. • Creating a parallel reporting workflow. •
Fully autonomous safety decision-making without expert validation. •
Requiring paid AI services. • Requiring a new data-collection system.

6.  TARGET USERS

Field Personnel Need: Continue reporting incidents and observations
without additional workflow burden. Value: Existing reporting remains
unchanged while earlier risk signals can trigger preventive action.

HSE Officers Need: Screen reports and identify high-priority risks
efficiently. Value: Causal reconstruction and prioritization support
evidence-backed intervention.

Site Leadership Need: Understand recurring safety weaknesses at site
level. Value: Recurring barrier failures become visible before
escalation.

OIL Management Need: Consistent risk prioritization across sites. Value:
Shared risk intelligence supports organization-wide safety decisions.

HSE Decision-Makers Need: Trust and validate AI-generated flags. Value:
Each flag carries causal evidence that can be reviewed and acted upon.

7.  CORE USER JOURNEYS

Journey A — Report to Risk 1. A UA/UC, near-miss, or incident report
enters PRAVAH. 2. NLP processing extracts relevant concepts from the
free text. 3. The system reconstructs the causal risk structure. 4.
Deterministic logic evaluates SIF potential and LSR relevance. 5. The
report is assessed for recurring precursor patterns. 6. PRAVAH generates
an explainable risk output. 7. HSE personnel review the result and take
appropriate action.

Journey B — Recurring Failure Detection 1. Multiple reports are
processed. 2. Semantically similar reports are connected even when
wording differs. 3. Common failure modes and precursor patterns are
surfaced. 4. HSE users can identify recurring barrier failures. 5.
Leadership can focus preventive interventions on recurring risks.

Journey C — Expert Feedback 1. An HSE expert reviews an AI-generated
output. 2. The expert validates or corrects the
classification/reasoning. 3. The correction becomes human-in-the-loop
feedback. 4. The shared engine uses feedback to improve future
performance.

8.  FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

FR-01: Safety Report Ingestion PRAVAH shall accept existing UA/UC
observations, near-miss reports, and incident reports as input.

FR-02: NLP Processing The system shall process free-text reports using
NLP techniques to identify relevant safety concepts and relationships.

FR-03: Causal Reconstruction The system shall represent report
information through the causal structure: Activity → Hazardous Energy →
Barrier Failure → Potential Consequence.

FR-04: SIF Potential Detection The system shall assess whether a report
contains potential Serious Injury & Fatality risk, including cases where
reported severity does not fully represent the potential consequence.

FR-05: Life-Saving Rule Mapping The system shall identify relevant IOGP
Life-Saving Rules and connect detected hazards/barrier failures to
applicable rules.

FR-06: Recurring Precursor Detection The system shall identify recurring
precursor patterns across reports.

FR-07: Semantic Failure Matching The system shall connect differently
worded reports when they represent the same underlying failure mode.

FR-08: Exposure-Weighted Prioritization The system shall support
exposure-weighted SIF density so that HSE teams can prioritize
concentrated fatal-potential risk rather than simply prioritizing
locations with more reports.

FR-09: Explainable Outputs Each significant flag should provide causal
evidence showing why the system identified the risk.

FR-10: Human-in-the-Loop Feedback The system shall allow expert
review/correction and use that feedback to calibrate the system.

FR-11: Existing Workflow Compatibility The system shall operate on the
organization’s existing safety reports without requiring a parallel
reporting process.

9.  PRODUCT OUTPUTS

For each analyzed report, the intended intelligence includes: •
Reconstructed activity. • Hazardous energy. • Identified or suspected
barrier failure. • Potential consequence. • SIF potential assessment. •
Relevant Life-Saving Rule. • Recurring precursor/failure pattern where
applicable. • Evidence supporting the generated flags. • Expert
review/correction status.

At an aggregated level, the system should surface: • Recurring failure
modes. • Recurring precursors. • Risk hotspots. • Barrier failures. •
Exposure-weighted SIF density.

on text similarity or classification.

14. FEASIBILITY AND VIABILITY

Feasibility principles: • One core causal structure supports three
critical decisions. • Existing HSSE reports are sufficient. • The core
can use open-source AI technologies, with optional APIs. • The
architecture is intended to scale from site-level deployment to
enterprise rollout.

Viability principles: • Convert reports into risk prioritization before
escalation. • Focus on underlying structure rather than wording. •
Preserve the existing workflow. • Use shared-engine feedback so one
expert correction can improve multiple decisions.

15. SAFETY AND TRUST REQUIREMENTS

Because PRAVAH supports safety-related decisions, outputs should be
treated as decision support rather than unquestionable autonomous
decisions.

The product should: • Show the causal evidence behind flags. • Allow
expert validation and correction. • Preserve a human-in-the-loop review
mechanism. • Use deterministic scoring/rules where specified for
critical logic. • Make it clear when an output represents potential risk
rather than confirmed consequence.

17. RISKS AND MITIGATIONS

Risk: AI produces an incorrect safety interpretation. Mitigation:
Human-in-the-loop expert review and explainable causal evidence.

Risk: Different reports are incorrectly grouped together. Mitigation:
Combine semantic similarity with structured causal reasoning and
deterministic rules.

Risk: High reporting volume is mistaken for high risk. Mitigation: Use
exposure-weighted SIF density and causal risk signals.

Risk: Users reject the system because it changes their reporting
process. Mitigation: Keep existing reporting workflows unchanged.

Risk: Lack of sufficient data. Mitigation: Product concept is designed
around existing HSSE reports and does not require new data collection.

Risk: Deployment cost becomes high. Mitigation: Use an open-source core
with optional APIs.

24. PRODUCT SUMMARY

PRAVAH is positioned as a causal risk intelligence layer over existing
safety reporting. Instead of treating each report as isolated text, it
reconstructs the underlying risk, connects differently worded reports to
common failure modes, and produces three critical decisions: SIF
potential, Life-Saving Rule relevance, and recurring precursor patterns.

Its central value proposition is:

Existing Reports → Causal Reconstruction → Explainable Risk Intelligence
→ Prioritized HSE Action

The product is designed around minimal workflow disruption, open-source
technology, deterministic safety logic, semantic NLP, and continuous
expert feedback.
